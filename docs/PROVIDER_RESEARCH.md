# Travel provider API research

**Research date:** 2026-10-17  
**Scope:** Official public documentation and access terms for transport, accommodation, activities, and booking referrals. This is a provider assessment, not an integration or a claim that accounts, contracts, inventory, or credentials have been obtained.

## Executive summary

There is no single public API that can currently be shown to satisfy the whole product. For an initial partner-dependent pilot, the smallest plausible set is:

1. **Flights: Duffel Flights**, subject to production account/contract review and validation that every candidate fare has sufficient baggage and seller data. It documents multi-slice shopping, offer retrieval/repricing and order creation. The API does not make every carrier or every bag combination complete.
2. **European rail and bus: Tictactrip**, subject to a commercial agreement and a written confirmation of route coverage, bag data, and handoff behavior. Its public developer page describes one API for live European rail/bus inventory and combined journeys; detailed public luggage and rate-limit guarantees were not found.
3. **Accommodation: Booking.com Demand API**, subject to Managed Affiliate Partner approval. It has date/occupancy/property search, current product prices and availability, hostel dormitory filters, and referral deep links. API coverage is explicitly not identical to all consumer-site inventory.
4. **Activities: defer in the first transport-first milestone.** If Booking.com grants access to its Attractions pilot, it is the lowest-vendor-count option. Otherwise Viator Full Access is a documented alternative after approval/certification. Basic Viator access does not provide live availability/pricing.

This is a **conditional recommendation**, not a statement that these providers have approved this project. Before an offer participates in a “cheapest” comparison, required baggage and mandatory fees must be known for the traveler’s requested bag configuration. “Search result retrieved,” “repriced,” “supplier booking readiness confirmed,” and “stale/unavailable/incomplete” must remain separate states. No provider documents global fare or route optimality.

Google Flights does **not** provide a generally available public structured-fare API for this use case in its current documentation. Google describes a confidential, invite-only partner proposal. A consumer Flights URL is a comparison/search destination, not an authorized offer feed or verified booking offer. We must not scrape it.

## A. Flights

### Duffel: leading candidate, with baggage and seller gates

Duffel’s [offer request API](https://duffel.com/docs/api/v2/offer-requests) accepts passenger objects and slices. A slice describes an origin, destination, and departure date; the concepts guide says a multi-city trip has more than two slices. That shape can represent one-way, return, open-jaw (different origin/destination airports), and multi-city searches. It does not mean every carrier supports every combination or date. Search coverage is dependent on the accessible airline content.

Offers contain price/currency, itinerary, owner/carrier information, conditions, and expiry metadata. Duffel describes offers as time limited (typically 30 minutes); retrieving/pricing an offer provides a current check but is not a guarantee that the airline will still accept it at order creation. Keep distinct states for initial search, repriced, supplier-ready, expired, unavailable, and incomplete. See [offer schema](https://duffel.com/docs/api/offers/schema) and [offer pricing](https://duffel.com/docs/api/offers/price-offer).

Baggage is a material limitation: included allowance can be present in the offer, but extra-bag services are carrier and offer dependent. Duffel’s [extra baggage guide](https://duffel.com/docs/guides/adding-extra-bags) says available services must be requested and supported by the airline. Unknown cabin/checked allowance or mandatory bag fee means that option cannot be ranked as a complete bag-inclusive price. Duffel also documents test cases where bag data is absent/variable ([test integration guide](https://duffel.com/docs/api/overview/test-your-integration)).

Duffel documents [order creation](https://duffel.com/docs/api/orders), which creates an airline booking and is a purchase path. Keep it unavailable until the traveler explicitly approves the itinerary and price. The offer’s owner/airline is not by itself sufficient to claim that an itinerary is booked directly with that airline; record and display the actual seller/merchant where returned, and otherwise mark seller/handoff details unresolved. Duffel’s public [pricing](https://duffel.com/pricing) lists $3 per confirmed flight order, $2 per paid ancillary, and a charge for searches above a stated search-to-book threshold, plus stated FX fees; verify current account terms at contracting time.

**Assessment:** reasonable initial API for actual multi-slice offers and reprice/order functionality, but only after account access, supplier-market review, seller identity, and bag-data coverage are proven. Duffel is an intermediary platform; include official airline links where a provider documents and exposes them, rather than promising that all offers hand off to an airline-owned booking page.

### Official airline booking route: Lufthansa Group partner APIs

Lufthansa Group’s [developer product page](https://developer.lufthansa.com/product) distinguishes open APIs from partner access for fare/deeplink functionality. The [Partner APIs page](https://developer.lufthansa.com/partner_apis) describes group airline fare and deeplink products. This is a possible official-site handoff for covered Lufthansa Group carriers, not a universal European or global flight search. NDC shopping/order management is a separate partner route described in the Group’s [NDC capabilities](https://lhgroupairlines.com/ndc/en/about-ndc/capabilities); public docs do not establish our eligibility, baggage completeness, commercial terms, or exact quotas. Obtain written confirmation before relying on prices or links.

### Google Flights: access conclusion

Google’s official [Flights Search documentation](https://developers.google.com/travel/flights) describes an initial program proposal shared confidentially under NDA with select, invite-only partners, including airlines and online travel agencies. This does not establish a public developer API or a generally available partner integration for our application. We have not verified an approved Google Flights API credential path for this use case.

The [consumer Google Flights page](https://www.google.com/travel/flights) is a user-facing comparison/search interface. A search URL can be a user navigation or comparison link, but it does not deliver structured, attributable offer data to our system and cannot support baggage-inclusive ranking, repricing, validation, or booking-readiness claims. Google’s separately documented [Travel Transport API](https://developers.google.com/travel/transport) is for transport partners to integrate with Google’s transport experience; it is not an inbound Google Flights fare feed. No scraping, browser automation, bot evasion, or access-control bypass is proposed.

### Amadeus: legacy Self-Service is not an access plan

The current [Amadeus API portal](https://admin.developers.amadeus.com/) says Self-Service was decommissioned on July 17 and the site is now for Enterprise APIs. It does not specify the year on the landing text, so we do not infer it. The former [Self-Service FAQ](https://admin.developers.amadeus.com/self-service/apis-docs/guides/developer-guides/faq/) and [pricing guide](https://admin.developers.amadeus.com/self-service/apis-docs/guides/developer-guides/pricing/) remain discoverable but are not proof of current onboarding or availability. Enterprise access is request/sales-led and exact search/reprice/order, baggage, fees, and coverage must be obtained from an Enterprise proposal. Do not build against old Self-Service examples as if they were currently provisionable.

## B. European rail and bus

### Tictactrip

**Implementation follow-up (2026-10-17):** The current [official OpenAPI reference](https://developers.tictactrip.eu/api) and [search guide](https://developers.tictactrip.eu/docs/search) expose stop autocomplete and a result schema containing per-segment station IDs, local timestamps/offsets, duration, mode/operator, bookability, fare cents, a coarse `includedBaggage` flag, and redirect references. The schema examples do not return currency; a euro example in the [glossary](https://developers.tictactrip.eu/docs/glossary) is insufficient to assume all account/market currency. Our adapter therefore requires a partner-confirmed configured currency to mark a fare complete. Luggage counts/limits/fees, protected-connection rules, and minimum transfer times remain insufficiently specified publicly. Partner access and actual seller approval are still unresolved. See [`GROUND_TRANSPORT.md`](./GROUND_TRANSPORT.md) for implementation behavior.

Tictactrip’s official [developer platform](https://developers.tictactrip.eu/) describes a single API combining European train and bus providers, multi-operator routes, live price and availability search, and booking/ticket issuance. It advertises 250+ carriers in 20+ European countries, with examples including national rail operators and intercity bus brands. Those are provider claims, not a guarantee that every requested city pair or operator is sellable to our account.

Access is partner-led: request credentials and commercial terms. Public documentation did not establish a universal luggage schema covering carry-on dimensions, checked baggage, per-segment mandatory fees, or traveler bag counts. Nor did we find public rate limits, commission/transaction pricing, or an SLA. Ask for written confirmation of route/carrier coverage, price freshness/revalidation behavior, booking seller identity, baggage fields and fees, cancellation/exchange restrictions, rate limits, and permitted link/booking flow. Do not label an itinerary baggage-inclusive until all required transport legs have complete rules and costs.

### Distribusion alternative

Distribusion provides public [API documentation](https://docs.distribusion.com/) and markets rail distribution through [Rail Solutions](https://www.distribusion.com/rail-solutions). Its materials describe API/white-label distribution, search and ticketing, post-booking changes, and a rail portfolio of 40+ carriers; its [company page](https://www.distribusion.com/about-us) describes bus, train, transfer and ferry suppliers. Production access, exact country/operator markets, fares/availability freshness, luggage fields, rates, limits, and SLA remain commercial questions. Consider it if Tictactrip fails route coverage or data-contract validation; neither provider should be chosen on marketing footprint alone.

## C. Accommodation

### Booking.com Demand API

The [Demand API prerequisites](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/getting-started/prerequisites) require Managed Affiliate Partner status, a signed agreement, Partner Centre setup, and authentication credentials. This is not a public self-service consumer API. The [accommodation search API](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/accommodations/search-for-available-properties) supports stay dates, occupancy/room parameters, location and property filters, rates and availability. Its docs include dormitory inclusion/exclusion, which is relevant to hostels. Results include product/room information, price, availability, policies, and referral URLs. The [price guide](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/accommodations/prices-accommodations) describes price/charge details and the need to use the correct product total/preview workflow.

Booking.com explicitly cautions that Demand API inventory is not a one-to-one mirror of all consumer-site content and that property/rate content can be incomplete relative to the website. Thus it is a defined partner inventory, not a complete global accommodation search. Public docs do not disclose our eventual commission, rate quota, or all display/branding restrictions; obtain them from the account manager. The documented [accommodation tutorial](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/accommodations/accommodation-tutorial) supports redirect/deep-link referral; prefer this for planning over taking payment or placing an API order.

### Expedia Rapid alternative

Expedia’s [Rapid Lodging overview](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/lodging?locale=en_US) and [shopping API](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/lodging/shopping/about-shopping-api?locale=en_US) document date/occupancy-based room shopping, live rates/availability and price checking before booking. Rapid is a B2B partner product with approval and [launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/setup/launch-requirements/lodging-launch-reqs); pricing, commission, quotas, and contractual scope are not publicly settled. Its overview cites a 700,000-property inventory, but hostel/dorm support for our requested use was not verified. It is an alternative if Booking.com access or coverage is inadequate, with greater launch/contract burden for this initial product.

## D. Activities and attractions

### Booking.com Attractions (conditional, beta)

The official [Attractions API overview](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/attractions/about-attractions) describes a beta for selected partners, subject to change. The [search API](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/attractions/search-attractions) exposes attraction products, prices and date-specific availability with documented date/query limits. The [details API](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/attractions/retrieve-attraction-details) exposes location and estimated duration for applicable tours. The [quick guide](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/attractions/attractions-quick-guide) sends the traveler to Booking.com to complete a booking.

Access is not implied by Demand accommodation access: request beta approval. Opening hours for a venue were not verified in the public API docs. Exact inventory geography, rates, commission, and quotas require approval/account terms. This is the smallest supplier count if approved, but must not be treated as an available capability before then.

### Viator fallback

Viator’s official [Partner API docs](https://docs.viator.com/partner-api/technical/) and [access-level guide](https://partnerresources.viator.com/travel-commerce/levels-of-access/) distinguish Basic, Full, and Full+Booking. Basic affiliate access can expose product/content and availability schedule, but is not live for availability and pricing. Full requires approval/certification and provides real-time availability/prices, with Viator referral checkout. Full+Booking has a separate approval/certification path for on-site transactions. Viator’s [affiliate page](https://partnerresources.viator.com/travel-commerce/affiliate/) states 300,000+ experiences in 2,500 destinations and no API sign-up cost for affiliate access; actual commissions and limits are account-dependent. API rate limits are quota-based and returned in response headers; handle throttling rather than assuming an unchanging public numeric limit.

Location and product/tour duration can be included in product content/itinerary data. Generic attraction opening hours are not guaranteed by the product API. For this app’s current-price requirement, Basic access is insufficient; Full approval is a gate. Use a Viator referral unless transaction capability has been approved and the user separately consents.

## Recommended initial provider set and sequencing

### Smallest viable conditional set

- **Flights:** Duffel, gated on successful live-mode access and offer-level seller and baggage validation; evaluate Lufthansa Group partner deeplinks as an official airline handoff for supported carriers.
- **European ground:** Tictactrip, gated on a commercial agreement and verified coverage/luggage fields. Keep Distribusion as the fallback evaluation, not a simultaneous integration until it solves a demonstrated gap.
- **Stays:** Booking.com Demand API, gated on Managed Affiliate approval; use referral links and expose its inventory limitations.
- **Activities:** leave out of the first transport-first release. Add Booking.com Attractions only after beta approval, or Viator Full after certification. Do not substitute Viator Basic when the feature promises current availability and prices.

This keeps the initial scope to three providers for transport and lodging, and potentially one more for activities. No API keys, partner status, supplier access, or current inventory are established by this research. If access gates fail, show that the capability is unavailable rather than returning invented or simulated offers.

## Data and product requirements implied by the research

1. Persist source/provider and actual seller, retrieval timestamp, currency, total price, price components, fare/room/seat conditions, returned availability, expiry, and validation status on every offer.
2. Keep “retrieved,” “repriced,” “booking readiness confirmed,” “stale/unavailable,” and “incomplete” distinct. A recently searched offer is not guaranteed bookable.
3. Model passenger count/ages, dates/flexibility, departure and return airports independently, open-jaw endpoints, and multi-city slices. Provider support is a runtime capability, not a product-wide promise.
4. Model bags per traveler and per segment. If a required allowance or mandatory fee is missing, mark total as incomplete and exclude it from verified cheapest comparisons. Never infer baggage policy from a marketing summary.
5. Record search coverage (providers, operators, geography, dates, unsupported modes and filters) beside route recommendations. “Cheapest found in searched coverage” is the maximum defensible claim; global optimality is not established.
6. Reprice/validate immediately before referral or any future booking handoff. Keep planning and purchase separate, and require explicit user approval before any order or payment action.
7. Use only provider-authorized APIs and documented referral links. Do not scrape consumer pages, automate Google Flights, or bypass access controls.
